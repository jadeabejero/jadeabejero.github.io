Call QA & Coaching Scorecard

A small support team records every call but nobody has time to review them. I set up a scoring rubric, reviewed three calls with AI-assisted transcripts, and turned the scores into coaching each agent can act on this week.

SCENARIO: 5-agent home-internet support team, ~250 calls/week TOOLS: Whisper transcripts · Claude · Google Sheets TIMEFRAME: Setup 1 day, then ~2 hrs/week

1 · The problem

The team lead can listen to maybe 5 calls a week, so coaching runs on gut feel. Customers who threaten to cancel get very different handling depending on who picks up, and nobody can say why some agents save more accounts than others.

~250 calls/week recorded ~2% ever reviewed No written scoring rubric Recordings already exist

2 · The scoring rubric I wrote

Built from the quality standards I worked under on U.S. telecom accounts · 100 points
AreaWhat a great call doesPoints
OpeningGreets, gives name, verifies the account before discussing details10
AcknowledgeRepeats the customer's actual problem back in their words, before any script line15
OwnershipSays "I will", not "they will"; no transferring blame to other teams15
TroubleshootingChecks the obvious causes in order; explains each step in plain words20
RetentionWhen cancel is mentioned: fixes first, offers a fair option, never argues15
AccuracyCorrect policy, correct credit amount, no promises the company can't keep15
CloseClear next step with a time, confirms the customer is satisfied, notes the account10

4 · Coaching notes each agent gets

COACHING — Agent 2 (call #A-118) Score 64 · Focus: retention order

What went well: You found the duplicate charge quickly and quoted the right refund amount.

One thing to change: When the customer said "I'm cancelling," you went straight to the retention offer. She didn't feel heard yet, so the offer sounded like a sales pitch. Next time, fix the charge first ("I can see the double charge, and I'm reversing it right now"), then ask if there's anything else making her want to leave.

Try this line: "Let me fix this first. If you still want to cancel after, I'll make that easy too."

Delivered in the weekly 1:1 · Re-checked on next week's calls
SUMMARY — to team lead Week 1 · 3 calls reviewed (pilot)

Team average 79/100. The pattern to fix first: retention offers come before the problem is solved, and that's where the lowest scores are. Verification is the second gap (one call skipped it, a security risk).

Recommended next step: score 20 calls a week (4 per agent) with the same rubric so the trend is real, not a guess from three calls.

Dashboard: one sheet, one row per call, weekly average per agent and per rubric area

5 · How it runs every week

  • Mon — Pull 20 recordings (4 per agent, picked at random plus any escalations)
  • Mon — Transcribe with Whisper; Claude does a first-pass score against the rubric
  • Tue — I listen to the flagged parts and correct every score the AI got wrong
  • Wed — Coaching note per agent: one win, one change, one line to try
  • Fri — Team-lead summary: averages, the pattern to fix, calls worth sharing as examples
What the team gets

Coverage goes from about 5 calls a week to 20, every agent gets specific coaching instead of general feedback, and the lead can see which habit is costing saves. This is a sample built on a realistic scenario, so the numbers above are illustrative, not results from a real client.
